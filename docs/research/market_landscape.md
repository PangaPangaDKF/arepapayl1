# ArepaPay: Market Landscape and Reality Check

Research date: 2026-11-15. Every claim has a source URL. **UNCONFIRMED** means I could not verify the claim against a primary source, or the sources I found disagree. Many of the 2026 sources are trade or crypto press, and their figures should be checked against primary documents (OFAC, Gaceta Oficial, BCV, FATF) before anyone relies on them.

The concept under review: a private, permissioned Avalanche L1 for Venezuelan micro-merchants. Merchants are paid in USDT/USDC with prices shown in bolívares. Licensed banks provide the on/off ramps. Transactions are encrypted. A multisig of the banks and the operator can view, trace and freeze funds ("centralized privacy"). The chain is never bridged to anything else, and lending/RWA products come later.

---

## 1. Does this exist? Closest analogues

**Short answer:** No live system found does all of these together: a permissioned chain for micro-merchants, stablecoin settlement, bank on/off ramps, encrypted transfers, and a consortium freeze key. The pieces do exist separately, but almost always for wholesale or institutional users rather than micro-merchants. The retail and merchant successes (Pix, M-Pesa, Pago Móvil) are not blockchains at all.

## 2. Venezuela reality check (as of November 2026)

### 2.1 Politics and sanctions: what changed in 2026
- **3 January 2026:** US forces captured Nicolás Maduro. Delcy Rodríguez holds presidential powers under a Supreme Court order. No election date had been set as of October 2026 ([House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-12211/), [CFR](https://www.cfr.org/global-conflict-tracker/conflict/instability-venezuela)).
- **US relations:** The US recognised Rodríguez as interim head of state, restored diplomatic relations in March 2026, and removed sanctions on Rodríguez (OFAC removal around 1 April 2026) and on the BCV ([Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-12211/), [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/04/recent-news-from-treasurys-office-of-foreign-assets-control-april-6/)).
- **Transition talks:** A fourth round of talks was set for 11–15 November 2026. The US hopes for a new TSJ and CNE in early 2027, with elections within six months after that ([search summary of CFR/Crisis Group](https://www.crisisgroup.org/latin-america-caribbean/andes/venezuela); exact quote **UNCONFIRMED**).
- **Other shocks:** Twin earthquakes on 24 June 2026 killed more than 5,500 people (later estimates about 6,500) ([Latin Times](https://www.latintimes.com/venezuela-2026-christmas-season-maduro-earthquake-recovery-600782)). Political prisoner counts disagree: Foro Penal says 884 as of 3 November ([Wikipedia: 2026 political prisoner release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_political_prisoner_release_in_Venezuela)).
- **Status:** The situation is still unstable. Institutions are interim, there is no electoral timetable, and civil liberties remain restricted.

### 2.2 OFAC rules relevant to Venezuelan banks
- **GL 57 (14 April 2026)** authorizes financial services involving the BCV, Banco de Venezuela, Banco del Tesoro, Banco Digital de los Trabajadores, and entities 50%+ owned by them. Covered services include accounts, loans, transfers, ACH/wires, cards, **digital wallets**, FX, and USD correspondent accounts ([Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/04/ofac-issues-general-licenses-authorizing-venezuelan-certain-commercial-and-banking-transactions/), [K&L Gates](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/ofac-eases-sanctions-on-financial-services-and-commercial-related-transactions-in-venezuela), [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/ofac-issues-new-venezuela-related-general-licenses-authorizing-commercial-negotiations-of-contingent-contracts-with-gov-and-financial-services-involving-named-venezuelan-banks/)).
- **GL 56** covers negotiation (only) of contingent contracts with the Government of Venezuela. **GL 58** (5 May 2026) covers debt-restructuring services. GLs 52–58 were published in the Federal Register on 30 September 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20008/publication-of-venezuela-sanctions-regulations-web-general-licenses-52-53-54-55-56-57-and-58), [OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/selected-general-licenses-issued-ofac)).
- **Still restricted:** The Government of Venezuela and PDVSA remain blocked, and the GLs are licenses, not a lifting of sanctions ([Agora Latam](https://www.agoralatam.com/insights/ofac-general-licenses-venezuela-april-2026)). Whether the BCV was formally delisted from the SDN list or only licensed is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **Private banks** (Banesco, Mercantil, Bancamiga, BNC, etc.) are not named in GL 57. They are generally not SDNs, but they face **de-risking**: international banks stay cautious even where transactions are permitted ([FTI Consulting, March 2026](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/lifeblood-recovery-venezuela-global-financial-system)).
- **Implication:** Partnering with a *state* bank brings government-ownership and PEP optics and depends on the GL staying in force. A private bank has cleaner optics but weaker correspondent access. Partnering with a US bank is unlikely: JPMorgan reportedly froze Kontigo/BlindPay accounts ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/markets/crypto-exchange-kontigo-suspends-us-accounts-for-venezuelans/)).

### 2.3 Regulators
- **BCV:** Its sanctions were lifted in April 2026 and its return to the IMF was backed ([MercoPress](https://en.mercopress.com/2026/04/15/us-lifts-sanctions-on-venezuela-s-central-bank-and-backs-its-return-to-the-imf)). Since June 2025, banks have sold USDT to businesses holding government-approved wallets ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/venezuelas-sanctions-stablecoins)). No formal BCV stablecoin regulation found (**UNCONFIRMED**).
- **SUDEBAN:** Said it was evaluating new payment methods including crypto, and gave banks 10 days to propose formulas ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/regulacion/regulador-bancario-venezolano-evalua-implementacion-pagos-criptomonedas/)). Conexus, which handles about 40% of electronic transfers, is building a bank app to custody USDT and expects the BCV and SUDEBAN to supervise it ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/mercados/venezuela-app-bancos-usdt-stablecoins/), [CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/87f06-venezuelas-conexus-to-integrate-bitcoin-and-stablecoins-in-banking-system)). Date of the SUDEBAN statement: **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **SUNACRIP:** Was paralysed after March 2023 ([Lightspark](https://www.lightspark.com/knowledge/is-crypto-legal-in-venezuela)) and is operating again in 2026: it issued the Crixto license in January 2026, and reportedly only Crixto and Kontigo are formally authorized, with licenses expiring between late 2026 and early 2027 ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/mercados/crixto-renovacion-operaciones-2026-venezuela/)). The legal basis is the 2018/2019 Constituent Decree on the Sistema Integral de Criptoactivos ([vLex](https://vlexvenezuela.com/vid/decreto-constituyente-sistema-integral-762056869)). Providencia 054-2022 sets AML rules for fintechs ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/ve/es/home/insights/2022/06/sunacrip-publica-la-providencia-054-2022-dirigida-a-las-fintech.html)). A proposed *Ley Orgánica de Economía Digital* was floated in October 2026 but has not been enacted ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/venezuela/venezuela-busca-regular-una-economia-digital-que-el-sector-estima-en-us80000-millones/)).
- **PDVSA-Cripto (2023):** SUNACRIP was used to market oil cargoes off the books. The superintendent Joselit Ramírez was arrested and Tareck El Aissami resigned and was later detained. Estimated losses range from $3B (initial prosecutor figure) to $16.96B (Transparencia Venezuela) to $23B. Trial of more than 60 defendants began in April 2026 ([BeInCrypto](https://es.beincrypto.com/que-es-trama-pdvsa-cripto-impacto-venezuela/), [La Patilla](https://lapatilla.com/2024/04/10/escandalo-pdvsa-cripto-el-modus-operandi-y-los-montos-desviados-por-la-red-criminal-de-tareck-el-aissami/), [CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/sucesos/caso-pdvsa-cripto-juicio-venezuela/)). **Lesson:** the crypto regulator itself was the laundering channel. A "trusted regulator plus operator" freeze key is exactly the governance that failed in Venezuela.

### 2.4 Exchange rate and inflation
- **Official rate:** about 301 VES/USD at the start of 2026, 859.06 at the end of September, and 889.26 on 30 October ([Venezuela Tribune](https://venezuelatribune.com/venezuela-inflation-slows-to-8-4-in-september-but-prices-are-still-up-466-in-a-year/), [Tepuy Ventures](https://tepuyventures.com/indicators)).
- **Gap to USDT/parallel:** about 65.7% in January 2026, 30–40% in June, about 12.3% in August (government figure) and about 11.7% in October ([Orinoco Tribune](https://orinocotribune.com/bcv-reports-3rd-straight-month-of-single-digit-inflation-in-venezuela), [Venezuelanalysis](https://venezuelanalysis.com/news/venezuela-monthly-inflation-hits-18-month-low-exchange-rate-gap-persists/), [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/venezuela-exchange-rate-gap-12-3-2026/)). The benchmarks differ across sources.
- **Inflation:** 8.4% month-on-month in September, 6.2% in October, about 465.6% year-on-year in September. The IMF's October 2026 WEO projects 467.9% average for 2026, the highest in the world ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/venezuela-inflation-september-2026), [Orinoco Tribune](https://orinocotribune.com/bcv-reports-3rd-straight-month-of-single-digit-inflation-in-venezuela)).
- **Implication:** Settling bank ramps at the *official* rate is costly for merchants while the gap is 10%+, and a narrowing gap shrinks the USDT premium (and the arbitrage). The design must work in both regimes.

### 2.5 How widespread USDT is among merchants
- Ecoanalítica (April 2025): 84.2% of transactions in bolívares and 10.2% in USD, with FX use mostly in cash ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/venezuela-esta-realmente-ante-una-rebolivarizacion-de-su-economia-el-dolar-sigue-fuerte/)). Older Caracas/Maracaibo samples showed more than 60% FX ([Diario Las Américas](https://www.diariolasamericas.com/america-latina/mas-del-65-las-transacciones-venezuela-se-pagan-moneda-extranjera-n4220267)).
- Merchants interviewed for Chainalysis received about 40% of income in USDT. This is anecdotal, not a survey ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/latin-america-crypto-adoption-2026/)).
- Stablecoins reportedly handle 47% of transactions under $10k (**UNCONFIRMED**, CriptoNoticias summary). USDT is about 80% of oil-sale settlement ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/expert-80-of-venezuelas-oil-revenue-comes-in-usdt/)).
- **Bottom line:** USDT is a mainstream *store of value and B2B/treasury tool* and is common in P2P trading. At the retail point of sale, the bolívar (via Pago Móvil and cards) still dominates, priced off a USD reference. There is no national count of merchants that accept USDT (**UNCONFIRMED**).

### 2.6 Rules on crypto payments and stablecoins
- Crypto is legal and regulated by SUNACRIP under the Constituent Decree. Exchanges and intermediaries must be licensed, and unlicensed activity can be fined ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/gobierno/regulacion/venezuela-regir-sistema-integral-criptoactivos)). A 2019 SUNACRIP remittance providencia capped fees at 15% and required an authorized platform ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/gobierno/regulacion/gobierno-venezuela-dicta-controles-remesas-criptomonedas)). A 2026 version is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- Banks sell USDT to approved business wallets (since June 2025). There is no comprehensive stablecoin statute (**UNCONFIRMED**).
- **Implication:** ArepaPay almost certainly needs a SUNACRIP license, or must operate under a licensee (Kontigo/Crixto-style), to custody or exchange USDT for merchants.

### 2.7 IGTF
- 3% on payments in foreign currency (cash) and in crypto other than the petro, under Decreto 4.647 of 2022, collected by Special Taxpayers ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/regulacion/tributan-pagos-bitcoin-venezuela-5-claves-entenderlo), [El Diario](https://eldiario.com/2022/02/03/impuestos-transacciones-en-dolares-y-criptomonedas/)). The 2024 decree set 0% for some bank payments between special taxpayers and kept 3% for others ([Acceso a la Justicia](https://accesoalajusticia.org/fijada-en-cero-por-ciento-0-la-alicuota-del-impuesto-a-las-grandes-transacciones-financieras-igtf/)). The Executive can set the rate between 2% and 20%.
- Compliance is patchy: most businesses surveyed did not charge it ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/regulacion/confusion-venezuela-impuesto-divisas-negocios-no-aceptan-dolares-bitcoin)). The IGTF contributed to a fall in cash dollars ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/venezuela-esta-realmente-ante-una-rebolivarizacion-de-su-economia-el-dolar-sigue-fuerte/)). A tax reform was announced in October 2026 for "before end-2026", content unknown ([El Diario](https://eldiario.com/2026/10/08/inicio-navidad-venezuela/)).
- **Implication:** Automatic tax deduction is a real selling point to SENIAT and regulators. For merchants who currently do *not* pay the 3%, it is a disincentive: ArepaPay would make their USDT receipts visible and taxable.

## 3. Perspectives

### (a) Old economy, central banks, BIS, IMF
- **BIS (AER 2025):** Stablecoins fail the tests of singleness, elasticity and integrity. Unhosted public-chain stablecoins carry AML and sanctions risk. The BIS favours tokenized deposits and tokenized reserves on a unified ledger ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)). HSBC Research disputes the singleness critique ([HSBC](https://www.business.hsbc.com/en-gb/insights/bis-and-stablecoins)).
- **IMF:** USD stablecoins can speed up currency substitution, get around capital controls and weaken monetary policy, and the risk is highest in high-inflation EMs ([IMF blog, December 2025](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2025/12/04/how-stablecoins-can-improve-payments-and-global-finance), [IMF speech, August 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/08/07/sp080726-stablecoins-emerging-markets-dan-katz), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-12-04-imf-warns-stablecoins-may-accelerate-currency-substitution-381467)).
- **Read-across:** A BCV with a returning IMF program has an incentive to *limit* USD-stablecoin merchant rails. It may prefer a bolívar deposit token or a bank-issued USD deposit token. ArepaPay's pitch to the BCV should present it as visibility into an economy that is already dollarized and as tax collection, not as more dollarization.

### (b) Bank compliance
- **FATF:** Venezuela has been grey-listed since June 2024 and was still listed after the June 2026 plenary ([FATF June 2024](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/fatf-gafi/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-june-2024.html), [FIAU Malta, June 2026](https://fiaumalta.org/news/fatf-public-statements-19th-june-2026/)). Status after the October 2026 plenary is **UNCONFIRMED** ([FATF October 2026 page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/fatf-gafi/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-october-2026.html)).
- The FATF's 2025 and 2026 targeted updates say stablecoins now account for most identified on-chain illicit activity. Travel Rule legislation covers 83% of surveyed jurisdictions (2026) ([FATF 2025](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/targeted-update-virtual-assets-vasps-2025.html), [Cyprus Gaming Commission summary](https://www.cgc.org.cy/en/fatf-issues-sixth-targeted-update-on-virtual-assets-and-vasps/)).
- **Bank view:** A closed chain with no outside visibility looks like a sanctions-evasion risk unless the bank holds full view rights. Banks will insist on KYC for *payers* too, not only merchants, once flows cross their ramp. Travel Rule data must travel with on/off-ramp transfers. De-risking is the default for Venezuela exposure ([FTI](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/lifeblood-recovery-venezuela-global-financial-system)).

### (c) Crypto-native critique of "centralized privacy"
- **"A private blockchain is just a shared database"** ([Princeton CITP](https://blog.citp.princeton.edu/2015/09/18/private-blockchain-is-just-a-confusing-name-for-a-shared-database/)). With a closed validator set run by the banks plus the operator, and no bridge, the chain adds little over a Postgres ledger with signed audit logs, apart from programmability and future optionality.
- **Freeze powers and trust:** Who freezes, under what legal basis, and how fast? The Circle and Tether split shows that both "too slow" and "too arbitrary" are failure modes ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-04-13-circle-ceo-allaire-defends-decision-not-to-freeze-usdc-in-drift-exploit-citing-moral-quandary-397180)). In Venezuela, the regulator was the thief (PDVSA-Cripto).
- **Honeypot:** A view-everything key plus a KYC database makes a target. The Coinbase insider breach in 2025 leaked KYC data on 69,461 users after support agents were bribed, which led to social-engineering theft ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/355216/coinbase-reveals-69461-users-affected-in-december-2024-data-heist-filing)). In a country with kidnapping and extortion risk, knowing which shop holds how many dollars is a physical-security issue.
- **Multisig does not equal decentralization:** Ronin's 5-of-9 multisig was compromised because one organization effectively controlled the threshold ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/axie-infinity-developers-made-some-trade-offs-that-enabled-625m-ronin-breach)).
- **Not really private:** eERC hides amounts but not counterparties ([Avalanche docs](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)).

### (d) The micro-merchant's practical view
- **Cost:** The rails merchants use today, Pago Móvil and Binance P2P, are cheap or free. Pix and Pochi show micro-merchants adopt only near-zero-fee rails ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy26-mpesa-results-explained/)). A 3% IGTF plus an official-rate spread is a 10%+ haircut, and it kills the proposition.
- **Connectivity and power:** Blackouts in 2026 hit Zulia, Carabobo, Lara and Caracas. Point-of-sale terminals fail and sales fall more than 90% to cash-only ([El Diario](https://eldiario.com/2026/08/04/apagones-zulia-comercio/), [Finanzas Digital](https://finanzasdigital.com/impacto-de-los-cortes-electricos-en-el-comercio-consecomercio/), [El Diario, 10 November 2026](https://eldiario.com/2026/11/10/fallas-internet-venezuela/)). An offline or deferred-settlement mode is needed.
- **Cash:** The stock of cash dollars fell from more than $8B to under $5B (November 2024 to April 2025) ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/venezuela-esta-realmente-ante-una-rebolivarizacion-de-su-economia-el-dolar-sigue-fuerte/)). Cash is still the fallback.
- **Trust:** Merchants remember the Petro, PDVSA-Cripto, and bank-account freezes. A freeze key held by banks and "the operator" reads as "the government can take my dollars".
- **Taxes:** Informal merchants avoid the IGTF. Automatic tax is a cost to them, not a feature, unless it comes with something they value (credit, formal invoices, protection).

## 4. Where this model is likely to fail, and where it could fit (ranked)

### Likely failure modes (most to least likely)
1. **Official-rate ramps make it uneconomic.** With a gap of 11–65% during 2026 and the 3% IGTF, merchants lose value versus Binance P2P ([Orinoco Tribune](https://orinocotribune.com/bcv-reports-3rd-straight-month-of-single-digit-inflation-in-venezuela)). Merchants route around it.
2. **No partner bank signs.** Grey-listing, de-risking, and GL dependence for state banks ([FTI](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/lifeblood-recovery-venezuela-global-financial-system)). Partior shows that even top-tier consortia add banks slowly.
3. **Licensing.** Custody and exchange of USDT require SUNACRIP authorization. Only about 2 firms hold it, and licenses expire late 2026 to early 2027 ([CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/mercados/crixto-renovacion-operaciones-2026-venezuela/)).
4. **The "closed chain" contradiction.** USDT/USDC cannot natively exist on a never-bridged private L1. What circulates is an IOU backed by USDT held by a custodian, which is a deposit-taking or e-money activity, and the issuer can still freeze the reserve ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/tether-freezes-182-million-in-stablecoins-as-reports-point-to-heavy-crypto-use-by-venezuela-2000709276)). This is a design-level truth, not a regulatory guess.
5. **Privacy tech cost and immaturity.** Drex fell from 150 to under 10 TPS with privacy ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20250529-emerging-technologies-lessons-pix-and-drex-and-future-perspectives)). Tessera was deprecated. eERC has no proven production users.
6. **Trust and governance optics.** The freeze key is held by banks and the operator in a country where the crypto regulator embezzled billions (PDVSA-Cripto).
7. **Infrastructure.** Blackouts and outages ([Finanzas Digital](https://finanzasdigital.com/apagones-prolongados-venezuela-2026/)).
8. **Lending stage.** Thin, short histories. Goldfinch-style off-chain fraud. Macro-driven defaults ([DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/defi/goldfinch-borrower-lend-east-defaults-says-warbler-labs/)).

### Where it could fit (most to least plausible)
1. **B2B restocking loops.** A distributor or wholesaler and its 50–300 retail clients already settle in USDT/USD. The closed loop is natural (supplier and buyers on the same ledger), payment history is credit-relevant, and the value of each payment justifies the setup.
2. **Formalization and tax-compliance tool for merchants who *want* to formalize** (Special Taxpayers, firms seeking bank credit). Automatic IGTF/IVA receipts and clean books are the value.
3. **Bank-side "USD deposit token" pilot.** A private bank issues tokenized USD deposits to merchant clients. This is the JPMD/Agorá model, which regulators prefer to raw USDT ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e3.htm)).
4. **Remittance-to-merchant corridor** (diaspora pays a shop directly for family groceries), if a licensed remittance partner exists.
5. **Consumer retail point of sale** is the weakest fit. Pago Móvil and Binance already serve it more cheaply.

## 5. Design implications for a pilot

1. **Legal wrapper first.** Operate under a SUNACRIP-licensed entity (partner with Kontigo/Crixto-type licensees, or apply), with a local Venezuelan SA and a non-US holding structure reviewed by sanctions counsel. Obtain a written OFAC analysis: no GoV/PDVSA counterparties, screening against SDNs, and whether a GL-57 state bank is used.
2. **Partner type to approach first:** **one mid-size private bank already integrating stablecoin fintechs** (Bancamiga, BNC, Activo, Plaza are already Kontigo partners per [CriptoNoticias](https://www.criptonoticias.com/mercados/kontigo-activa-conversion-de-dolares-a-usdc-en-banco-nacional-de-credito-de-venezuela/)), plus **one wholesale distributor** that brings its merchants. Don't start with state banks (political optics, GL dependency) or US banks (de-risking).
3. **Who holds the auditor/freeze keys:**
   - Separate **view** (audit) keys from **freeze** keys.
   - Freeze should be a threshold (for example 3-of-5) across *independent legal entities*: partner bank compliance, operator, an independent custodian or law firm, and optionally a merchant-association representative. No single organization should control the threshold (the Ronin lesson).
   - Write down a public freeze policy: triggers (court order, SUDEBAN/UNIF request, verified theft report), time limits (for example a 72-hour temporary hold that lapses without a court order), an appeal path, and logging of every use.
   - Use HSMs/MPC for the keys, rotate the eERC auditor key, and allow decryption only per case, not bulk.
   - Keep the KYC database off-chain, minimized and segregated, given the honeypot and physical-security risk.
4. **Be honest about "privacy".** eERC hides amounts only. Restrict RPC and explorer access on the permissioned L1 to hide the payment graph from the public, and state plainly that the consortium sees everything.
5. **Settlement asset.** Choose and document one: (a) a custodied-USDT IOU (you become a custodian or e-money issuer), or (b) a bank-issued USD deposit token (the bank's balance sheet, regulator-friendly). Plan for issuer freezes of the reserve.
6. **FX policy.** Don't force official-rate conversion for merchant receipts. Hold USD-denominated balances and convert only on demand at a transparent, disclosed rate. Measure the spread merchants actually pay against Binance P2P.
7. **Offline tolerance.** QR plus signed offline vouchers or deferred settlement, and an SMS/USSD fallback for balance checks.
8. **Don't build a chain before you need one.** Run phase 0 on a centralized ledger with signed audit logs, and switch to the L1 when the bank wants node or validator rights. This de-risks the Drex/Tessera trap.
9. **Lending later and off-chain.** Credit only after 12 or more months of data, through a licensed lender, with losses held off-chain and checks of off-chain conduct (the Tugende lesson).
10. **Metrics to measure:**
   - active merchants per week (not sign-ups);
   - repeat payment rate;
   - share of merchant revenue processed;
   - all-in cost versus Pago Móvil and Binance P2P (spread + IGTF + fees);
   - on/off-ramp time to settle;
   - downtime during blackouts;
   - number of freeze/view requests, the time taken and the outcome;
   - support tickets about trust or freezes;
   - tax collected automatically against the merchant's prior declared amount;
   - churn reasons;
   - for credit pilots, 30/90-day delinquency against a control group.
